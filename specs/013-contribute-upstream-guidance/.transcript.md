# Session Transcript: Contribute Upstream Guidance

## Session Overview
The two askfirst-generated messages that hint a capability might belong upstream ("...the capability may belong in `{pkg}` itself") were vague and unattributed. The session added optional `contribute_how`/`contribute_url` fields to `askfirst_init()` so maintainers can supply concrete contribution guidance, replacing the generic framing with a named, attributed invitation.

## Design Decisions Made

### Decision 1: Scope limited to two message points
**Chosen:** The new invitation text replaces wording in `askfirst_build_notice()` (load-time) and `askfirst_build_scenario_check_message()` (scenario-check) only. `askfirst_capability_gap()` (fully author-supplied message today) and `error_redirect` (deliberately reuses raw `notice` text verbatim, stage 003) are out of scope.
**Rationale:** These two are the only askfirst-generated boilerplate carrying the vague framing today; the other two signal types either have no askfirst-added text to replace, or an established reason to stay verbatim.
**Proposed by:** joint

### Decision 2: No per-call override on `askfirst_capability_gap()`
**Chosen:** Contribution guidance is registered once per package via `askfirst_init()`, not overridable per capability-gap call.
**Rationale:** Moot in practice since capability_gap is out of scope per Decision 1; kept simple for a possible future extension rather than adding parameters now.
**Proposed by:** joint

### Decision 3: Fallback always names askfirst, even with neither field set
**Chosen:** A base sentence ("The developers of `{pkg}` use the 'askfirst' system...") is always present; the "how"/"where" sentences are additive, not required for the attribution itself.
**Rationale:** Avoids ever reverting to the old fully generic, unattributed wording, even for adopters who haven't set the new optional fields.
**Proposed by:** joint

### Decision 4: No unqualified second-person "you" in the new text
**Chosen:** Every sentence names its addressee explicitly (e.g. "the human user"); the originally-proposed "You are invited to contribute" phrasing was rejected.
**Rationale:** The calling agent is the direct reader of this message text, not a human reading over its shoulder. An unqualified "you" defaults to being parsed as addressing the agent itself — actively wrong here, since the invitation is for the human, and a misread invitation could plausibly lead an agent to open an upstream PR unsupervised, which is the opposite of what askfirst exists to prevent.
**Proposed by:** git-user (identified during plan review, after the initial draft had already used "You are invited...")

## Tradeoffs Considered
- Reintroducing per-call contribution overrides on `askfirst_capability_gap()` vs. package-level-only guidance: package-level-only won, since the feature was already out of scope for that signal type this stage.
